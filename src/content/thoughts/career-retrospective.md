― Peter F. Hamilton, Mindstar Rising

My name is Ryan Roberts. I'm 26 years old, originally from Long Beach, California, and for the last 5 years, I have been living in Tel Aviv, Israel working as full stack software engineer. At leastI was working full time--- until just 2 weeks ago, where I left my most recent position at an early stage startup in the AI Native workspace industry (spring.new). I left the team on good terms, I'm just simply taking a short break 🧘‍♂️.

I never really shared my full story with anyone on how I got to where I am, so I'm using this time to do so. Who knows, maybe one person reading this might find this story interesting.

## The origins

Growing up in Southern California, I had a relatively good childhood growing up. Nothing went horribly wrong, and from an outside observer looking in, I had an ideal childhood.
Playing with lego, going on camping trips, ski trips with the family, playing RuneScape, having good friends--- all of the experiences I had in my youth bring me great love and joy.

## Okay lets get real

While my life seemed all happy and normal the outside, for the _longest time_ I can remember, I have always felt extremly detached and disonnected. Disconnected from myself, from reality, and from others. I have _stark memeories_ of me at **4 years old**, sitting in pre-school, having **intense** feelings of **derealization** and **depersonalization** (words I did not learn until much later). I was feeling absolutely spaced out, and was convinced that everything around me is not real. I thought these feelings were totally normal, and I don't think my infant brain could even comprehend what was supposed to feel "normal" or not.

The feelings of detachment only worsened over time. Around the time I turned 9, my performance in school started to **drop dramatically**.

From once being a top performer in my school, to soon be struggling to keep up with even the most basic of subjects---my mind was slipping, and I didn't know how or why.

In school, more than having good grades, _all I wanted_ was to be liked by others. To fit in, and to feel loved. The thing is, is that **I have always had all these things**. I'm not sure where this deep feeling of insecurity and unrest came from, but it only metastized and grew worse as time went on.

I cruised through middle-school, spending most of my playing video games (the 2011-2014 era for gaming was the best, no debate), I even had my own minecraft server at one point, when the game was in Alpha.
Enter high-school, and my horrible grades and poor performance continued. When I was 15, I got **heavily involved** in drugs. Weed was very common place in my school, but I decided to take things a step forward and abuse benzodiazapines, opaiates, lean, adderall, and more than anything---psychdelics.

## Buying drugs off the dark web

When I was 16, I got really fascinated with DMT. At the time I was reading lots of Alan Watts and Terrence McKenna. I read The Spirit Molecule by Dr. Rick Strassman, and I started to develop an overall fascination with this chemical.

Since I did not know where to buy this drug, I learned about the dark web, and how it's a place where people can buy and sell drugs. My main concern was getting anything sent to my house, so I purchased a P.O. box next to my high school (paying only in cash, to ensure I was not traced), and had basically a private mailbox for $20 a month. Also the main thing about dark market, is that you cannot pay in normal currency---You have to use Bitcoin.

Keep in mind this was in 2016 before Bitcoin became the commodity that it is today, so buying it was more difficult. There was a website called LocalBitcoins, where you could essentially find a local 'bitcoin dealer' to allow you to pay them cash in exchange for bitcoin.

So that's exactly what I did. I found and contacted the top rated bitcoin seller in my area, and he wrote to me saying he only accepted $200 minimum. I agreed to buy $400 worth of bitcoin from him.
Since at the time, I thought bitcoin was only meant for people to buy drugs and do illegal shit, I was skeptical about meeting this random guy in public. He also did not have a profile picture, and an Arabic name like Ahkmed Abjul. I made sure to bring a friend with me for the bitcoin deal, and told him to meet me in a Starbucks in a busy public area.

We agree on meeting at 2:00pm, and I'm sitting wating inside this cafe at 1:50. A few moments go by, and it's 2:00pm. It feels like I'm in an episode of Breaking Bad--- the part where Walter White is sitting in Los Pollos Hermanos waiting to meet Gus (the largest meth kingpin), and he doesn't know exactly what he looks like or who to look for.

Seconds pass, and I see what looks like a Rolls Royce drive up to the parking lot. A young, clean looking guy steps out of the car, and he looks like he could be in his early 20's. He's wearing gucci, prada sunglasses, and just looked like a gangster. I knew this had to be the guy.

I give him a head nod, and he gives me one back. He comes over and sits at my table, and asks me for the money. I take out the crumpled $400 in various amounts, and pass it to him. He counts it slowly, and makes sure it's all there. After he made sure the money was solid, he asks me to take out my phone, and show him my bitcoin wallet QR code. I show it to him, and he scans my QR code, and in moments I see a notification that xx just sent me bitcoin. He kept a small fee for his service, but right then and there--- sitting in a Starbucks cafe in 2016--- I had $400 of bitcoin. (For those who don't know, 1 Bitcoin in 2016 was worth around $400, and today it's worth over $97,500)

On top of requiring bitcoin to purchase drugs, I downloaded Tor, and discovered the links to the `.onion` dark market sites pretty quickly through Reddit forums. This was around 2016-2017, where major markets like SilkRoad were shut down, however new alternatives were popping up, like Alpha Bay and Dream Market.

I chose AlphaBay, and found a DMT dealer that had a high rating. On the dark market, you cannot write anyone in plain text, so you have to PGP encrypt your messages such that only the person you're sending it to can read your message.

I ordered about 0.02 bitcoin worth of DMT, and began to check my PO box every day.
It's always empty, until about 1 week later--- I see an envelope from "Morton's Salt Company". I took it home, and opened it the envelope. Inside was a letter saying 'We offer you to try a sample of a new salt formula we are creating!', and at the bottom, there was a package of white 'salt' stapled to the paper. The stealth was pretty brilliant, I have to say.

## Back to the main story

Basically from the ages of 15-17, I was constantly under the influence, and never went more than 2 days without altering my conciounsness on _something_. While most kids around me were concerned about which college they were going to, I was focused on what specific combination of chemicals I could take to unlock new doors of perception in my brain.

There was never anyone particular that played a bad influence on me--- the reality was that **I was the bad influence** amongst all my peers. Parents did not want me to be around their kids, because all I wanted to do usually involved doing illegal shit.

I was constantly getting in trouble at school, and was on a first-name basis with my school's police officer. I was extremly self-destructive, and continuously would destroy the trust and friendship with everyone around me, and anyone that came close to me. I was on a rapid downward spiral.

I have always had strong passions for music, art, and life, but I always defaulted to centering my personality around being a self-destructive and habitual drug user. I think it's because I felt this could make me feel more accepted and liked from others (so so stupid!).

## Okay bro, no one asked for the trauma drump.

Very shortly after me finishing high school (I don't think i ever received a certificate of completion, as I legitimately failed all of my classes), it's the summer of 2017, and in classic Ryan Roberts fashion, I invited some close friends of mine to do a **very high dose** of LSD (around 700 micrograms) at a beach around 40 minutes away from my home. The trip lasted for around 24 hours, and while I don't remember having any major takeaways about the trip, almost instantaneously after the trip ended, the priorities in my brain shifted. I started to clean up my act, and slowly developed a new attitude about life.

It could have just been me being on the verge of 18 years old, that I started to realize that spending all of my free time smoking weed was not the most ideal way to spend my time. Drugs no longer appealed to me, and at the time, the main things I was passionate about (and always have been passionate about)--- music, working out, eating clean--- were the main things occupying my headspace and was what I was determined to keep pursuing.

## The Programming Journey begins--- sort of?

It's September of 2018, and I'm 19 at the time. I'm working at a supermarket making minimum wage, bagging groceries for old ladies and collecting shopping carts in the 95°F Southern Californian heat.
There is kind of an unspoken rule in my parent's house: either go to college and study _anything_, or leave. Since I did not have money move out (as much as I would have wanted to), I took the college direction and enrolled at my local community college. Since I did not have a clear idea of what I wanted to study, I started taking any and all random classes under the sun- from Music Theory, to Philosophy, Honors English, Health & Nutrition, Weather and Climate, History, and weight training just to name a few. The whole idea of taking random courses at college really upset me, mainly because I did not have a clear idea of what I wanted to major in.

I also tried to fill out a form to receive financial aid, but after countless hours of making no progress (the form is literally a 200 page online questionarre), I ended up paying for all of my studies out of my own pocket. I remember it costing me around $600 a semester, and this was not including any of the required textbooks. To say I was financially irrisponsible and making poor judgement would be an understatement. One of the main disagreements I had in my household was me not understanding why I had to take random classes I did not care about; instead of simply spending more time to work and save up money and only enrolling in college once I had a major in mind- but there was no progress in finding a middle ground there. It was simply: be a full time student in college, or leave the house.

So that is what I did. I continued taking random classes for about a year, until I started to really hone in on what I wanted to spend all my time doing. I came across a few clickbaity youtube videos showing 'Day in the life as a software Engineer working at xx company!'. I did not know much about software development at the time, other than that writing code made up a large portion of the job. A lot of these videos seemed pretty appealing- eating free food all day, cozy offices, listening to music, relaxed dress code, high salaries--- What's there not to like? Without knowing anything about the profession, I kind of made the 2-second decision that this was the career path I wanted to pursue.

My community college (Long Beach City College) offered a 2-year program for getting an 'Associates Degree' in Computer Science. It's a degree that could have it's credits transferred over to any university such that I could complete the remaining credits to receive a 4 year degree.

The required coursework for this degree ranged from topics like Calculus 1+2, Physics, OOP in Java, and Computer Architecture, to name a few.

Keep in mind, I completed high school with maybe a 2.0 GPA, and the idea of taking Calculus just seemed out of the realm of possibilities for me. I did not get too discouraged about it though, and I just kind of viewed these required courses as _what I needed to do_ in order to move ahead in life.

So that's what I did. For the next year or so, on top of working full time as a professional grocery-bagger, I started grinding on computer science.

I would spend all my free time getting myself caught up with math using Kahn Academy (amazing platform), and just immersing myself and manifesting that **I will** become a programmer-- no matter what.

However, throughout the whole time of me going down the _traditional_ route of getting a formal computer science degree at a college seemed absolutely unproductive to me. My end goal was simply to become an employable software engineer, and the idea of taking calculus and physics at a college (as important as they are), did not seem at all like actionable steps towards achieving my goal. I really always valued education, I just did not see the value in paying for a degree, and learning outdated theoretical coursework with little-to-no practical value.
These negative thoughts I had on college would soon be affirmed- but until then, I kept my head down and grinded, ultimately just to try me best to satisfy my parents dreams of me being a good kid.

## We're almost done with the college part

My first exposure to _real_ programming was an **Object Oriented Programming in Java** course I took.

It was an absolutely miserable experience, the teacher was very apathetic, running through random Java code snippets without providing any practical use cases. Our final capstone project worth 90% of our grade was to a build a tic-tac-toe game that ran in the CLI. I managed to slop together a few hundred if-statements inside a single file, and in the end - I managed to have something playable.

At the end of this course, I started to become very discouraged with continuing down the traditional path of getting a computer science degree; mainly for how un-practical and outdated everything we were learning felt to me.

The straw that broke the camels back for me was the next CS class I took: CS15 - Computer Architecture. I'm not even joking, the professor (Mr. Zho) barely spoke any english, and wrote with the most unreadable handwriting on a chalkboard about how assembly language and compilers works, in a class with 70+ students. I remember sitting in class thinking 'What the actual fuck am I doing here'. No one around me had any clue what was going on, but everyone seemed recalcitrant in the process of continuing their degrees.

## Forget college. Time to do my own thing.

A few months into my CS track, I came across a youtube video titled 'the FASTEST way to become a software developer'. It was from a youtuber Aaron Jack. There are probably at least 10 videos with a similar title that get uploaded every day, but bro (who was a frontend at Uber) seemed to have solid advice. His advice was pretty straightforward--- Learn frontend web development, and get really good at it.
His outline was as follows:

1. spend a few months learning HTMl+CSS - get to a point where you can recreate any website pixel-to-pixel with just HTML+CSS
2. Learn JavaScript. Add a few JavaScript interactive elements to your websites
3. Go all in on React.
4. Start applying

Without knowing much, i said to myself: "I'm going to follow this guy's advice". I even left a comment on the youtube video at the time stating my progress, saying something like "I'm gonna follow this video and hopefully I'll land a software developer job in one year from now"
And without making this up, literally one year after the time I left that comment, **i got my first job offer as a full stack engineer**.

Backing up a little while before that though- while I was still taking courses at the college, and spending my hours listening to Mr. Zho speak give lectures in Chinese, I started to follow Aaron Jack's advice and decided to take a Udemy course on [advanced CSS and SCSS](https://www.udemy.com/course/advanced-css-and-sass/) from Jonas Schedmtmann. This is the first time where things started to click for me. The course was essentially 40+ hours of hand writing SCSS (every developer's dream - i know), ultimately creating some pretty breathtaking websites, even by today's standards. I even had one of my old portfolio website's from one of the websites I built in the course [see it here](https://old-resume.vercel.app/).

I think one of the main reasons that this course clicked for me, was just how practical everything was. Even though we were just writing rudimentary stylesheets, the instant feedback and visible artwork the computer was producing after each line code really spawned the beginning of my career working as a programmer.

I thought to myself, I'm going to finish this bullshit Associates Degree for Computer science (just for my family's sake), and enroll in a coding bootcamp afterwards, and just go full time in trying to get a job.
At the end of the day, I only had to complete a few more courses in order to finish the degree, so it seemed like a feasible plan. I even told a few of my classmates about my plan, and everyone looked at me as if I was an alien visiting from a different dimension. "What do you mean you're not going to get a 4 year degree??"- was the most common response I got. The idea of me taking this _slightly alternative_ route did not seem to compute with anyone in my classes. With all love though, I hope all of my previous classmates all have landed very fulfilling careers. I just simply could not follow the pack.

## Entering Monk Mode

Shortly after COVID-19 hit, there reached a point in my life that I call 'monk' era. Without getting too deep, I became massively depressed (even more so than I already am), with probably the most **intense feelings of derealization and depersonialization** I have ever felt. It was so bad to the point where every day I would have auditory hallucinations (usually things like hearing a loud train in the distance), and everything around me would appear hyper-real and HD. Without having really any friends or social life, I tried my best to channel my inner mental chaos into moving myself foward. Waking up at 5-am, taking cold showers, listening to David Goggins and Jocko Willink religiously, working out 2+ hours a day, taking CS classes at my community college, working late hours at the grocery store, and coming home every night to grind on web development--- this was my daily routine for around 6 months. At the time I was also strictly living a straight-edge vegan lifestyle, which for me involved no caffeiene, no drugs or alcohol, and cooking whole food plant based meals everyday.

A average day consisted of me:

- Wake up at 5 a.m., freezing cold shower, and having deep uninterruped focus working through a programming text book. (I was enjoying working through Python Crash course)
- Go to college from 7:00-11:00 (I made sure to enroll in the earliest classes possible to still be able to work after).
- Go to work at the supermarket (my shifts were usually 12-7, 1pm-8pm, and my least favorite: 3pm-12am)
- Go to the gym after work, high intensity weight training for at least an hour.
- Come home - to do homework and continue grinding on programming.

This routine gave me purpose, gave me fuel. As dark as my head was mentally,

As I continuined my grind, my sister told me about a program to do a full stack coding bootcamp in Tel Aviv, Israel. It was part of this organization called MASA. I have never been to Israel before, I have no connections to the land, but hearing about it sounded like a cool thing to do.
I desperately wanted to leave my hometown, and to also advance in my path towards becoming an employable software engineer, so I applied.

## Moving to Israel! 🌎

So I applied to the coding bootcamp and got accepted (after passing a suprisingly intense live-coding interview, requiring me to connect a google maps API to an existing web app) and I packed my suitcase preparing for the move. The only Hebrew word I knew at the time was שלום (Shalom), and I genuinely had no idea what there was in Israel. I just assumed that there were people riding camels everywhere, and that the country was an active warzone. Only the second part proved to be accurate.

After arriving, the whole country was in a lockdown, and I stayed in a hostel where food was taken to my room. The bootcamp was almost entirely remote due to Covid, and it occupied the majority of first 3 months living in Israel. The bootcamp itself was extremely poorly organized, but at the end of the day, I gained value from it due to the large amount of projects I built. The bootcamp was basically structured like this:

- A really shitty lecture on Zoom about some web development topic
- A take home assignemnt to implement what the lecturer taught about.

It was mostly self-learning, which I actually think is the best way for anyone to learn software development. In the end I built around 10-15 web apps, touching all parts of the modern web ecosystem (the MERN stack was all the rage at the time, and that was what was focused on in the bootcamp). I did not _learn_ many new things in the course, thanks to all the time I spent before self-studying, but in the end it was a good experience being forced to grind on building projects for 3 months straight. Also being in a new country was fun, and it kind of further forced me to take it seriously.

Shortly after finishing the course, I got referred from the instructors to join a pre-seed data science startup--- they were looking for a developer to help them build a project in the Fiverr/U pwork / gig-economy industry.

I accepted and joined them, and built a dashboard for fiverr freelancers for them to obtain analytics about their profile, gig rating, and competition. I used some pretty modern tech at the time (2021 we're talking here):

- React (bootstrapped with create-react-app 🤮) + Styled Components
- Web Scraping with Python BS4, Selenium, and a rotating mobile proxy service Luminati (Now BrightData)
- AWS Lambda endpoints with Python (Flask) + Chalice (A CLI tool for deploying python lamdba endpoints)
- AWS Amplify for deploying and hosting the app.

After the contract ended, the company was not able to hire me, but they said I did a great job. I was at a crossroads- to stay in Israel, or to return to the US. I said to myself- fuck it, I'm already here, let's try to apply for a proper full stack engineer role. After lots of rejections, I landed a very great job at an ecommerce startup, CityHive, where I worked my ass off for almost 3 years. Since then, I have worked at various startups in size, built countless side projects, gave lectures, and so much more in-between.

After being in the industry for 6 yers, I feel like my journey is only just beginning, and there is so much more to accomplish.

My main focus recently has been on Modal - an AI inference company that I've been using on a personal project.

I don't have much more words to add right now, but if you made it this far- I hope this was an interesting read 🙏

There is so much more in between and crazy shit that has happened, from being drafted to the IDF, to joining the Israeli hardcore and metalcore music scene, so maybe I'll make a part 2.
